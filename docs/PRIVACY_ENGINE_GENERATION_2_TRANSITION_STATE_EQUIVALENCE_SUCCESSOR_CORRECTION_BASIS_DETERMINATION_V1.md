## 7. Authoritative-derivation determination

Generation 2 establishes that:

- authorization is bounded to identified `Q0`;
- `Q0` contains protocol-relevant state required to define and validate `U`;
- the applicable current protocol state must be compared with authorized `Q0` before execution and commitment;
- material conflict makes authorization stale;
- stale authorization prohibits commitment against the conflicting state;
- an implementation must not select or substitute a preferred state;
- stale authorization does not rewrite the earlier Evaluation or Determination;
- re-evaluation, where required, creates new occurrences.

Generation 2 does not determine:

1. which protocol-state elements form the comparison projection;
2. how those elements are canonicalized;
3. whether equivalence means exact state equality, precondition equivalence, transition-read-set equivalence, invariant equivalence or another relation;
4. which differences are immaterial;
5. which differences constitute material conflict;
6. which authoritative object defines the relation;
7. how the relation is bound to `T`, `U`, `Q0` and the transition authorization;
8. the complete authoritative comparison inputs;
9. how unavailable, invalid, unauthenticated, ambiguous or incomparable state is classified;
10. whether and how comparison, authorization or evaluation may be retried.

Generation 2 references predicates including:

- `equivalentTransitionState(Qx, Q0)`;
- `equivalentPreState(Q0a, Q0b)`.

It does not completely define either predicate.

The statement that `Q0` contains the protocol-relevant state needed to define and validate `U` constrains a future rule. It does not enumerate or uniquely derive the projection, comparison predicate or materiality boundary.

Exact whole-state equality cannot be inferred because Generation 2 limits comparison to the protocol-relevant purpose of `U`.

A narrower equivalence relation cannot be inferred because Generation 2 supplies no unique projection or materiality rule.

Therefore:

`transition-state equivalence is referenced but not normatively completed`

The missing rule cannot safely be treated as implementation-defined.

## 12. Deterministic three-result classification

Every complete comparison attempt must produce exactly one of three mutually exclusive results.

### 12.1 equivalent

`equivalent` means:

- every required input is authoritative, available, valid and canonicalizable; and
- the authoritative current protocol-state projection satisfies the protocol-defined equivalence relation to the authorized `Q0` projection.

### 12.2 material-conflict

`material-conflict` means:

- every input required to determine the relevant difference is authoritative, available and valid; and
- a complete comparison establishes at least one protocol-defined material conflict.

### 12.3 indeterminate

`indeterminate` means:

- equivalence cannot be established because at least one required input, authority relation, canonicalization operation, validation operation or comparison operation is unavailable, invalid, unauthenticated, ambiguous, incomplete or otherwise non-determinable.

The three result classes are exhaustive and mutually exclusive.

`indeterminate` must not be treated as `equivalent`.

`indeterminate` must not be treated as `material-conflict`.

Absence of established equivalence must not be interpreted as proof of material conflict.

Absence of established material conflict must not be interpreted as equivalence.

## 13. Fail-closed gate behaviour

Only `equivalent` may permit execution or commitment.

A `material-conflict` result must:

- classify the authorization as stale against the conflicting state;
- prohibit commitment of `U`;
- prohibit repair, substitution, widening, narrowing or implementation-selected reactivation;
- preserve the earlier Evaluation and Determination unchanged;
- preserve the identity and history of the affected authorization and `U`.

An `indeterminate` result must fail closed.

Before execution begins, `indeterminate` must:

- prohibit entry into bounded execution;
- prohibit commitment;
- produce the existing pre-transition-failure semantics.

After bounded execution has begun but before commitment, `indeterminate` must:

- prohibit commitment;
- prohibit partial commitment;
- produce transition-failure semantics.

An `indeterminate` result must not by itself:

- classify the authorization as stale;
- imply Protocol Action rejection;
- imply Evaluation failure;
- imply an incorrect Determination;
- create an alternative transition;
- create an authoritative successor state.

A terminally failed `U` must not be resumed, repaired, reinterpreted or converted into a committed occurrence.

## 14. Retry, reauthorization and re-evaluation

Each applicable transition mapping must normatively select its recovery disposition.

The permitted successor-defined dispositions are:

- authorization of a new `U` from the unchanged earlier `D`; or
- a required new Evaluation, new Determination and new transition occurrence.

The governing mapping must select the applicable disposition.

The implementation must not select between them.

Any later occurrence must have an identity distinct from every earlier occurrence.

Retry, reauthorization or re-evaluation must not mutate, repair, replace or silently reinterpret an earlier:

- Evaluation;
- Determination;
- transition occurrence;
- authorization;
- comparison result;
- failure classification.

Where new Evaluation is required, it must produce a new Determination and, where applicable, newly authorized transition occurrences.

The successor correction must require the commitment comparison to apply to the authoritative protocol state at the semantic commitment boundary.

A comparison followed by an intervening authoritative protocol-state change does not establish equivalence at commitment.

Comparison validation and commitment must form one indivisible protocol-semantic decision.

The correction must preserve:

`protocol-semantic atomicity != implementation transaction atomicity`

The correction must not require or elevate any particular:

## 16. Privacy and observability constraints

The successor correction must preserve:

`Private actors, public actions.`

The equivalence comparison must use only the minimum protocol-state projection required by the governing transition mapping.

It must not introduce or expose:

- Private Actor identity;
- enrollment or admission provenance;
- reusable participant identity;
- identity continuity across bounded actions;
- cross-action linkage;
- an actor-reconstruction bridge;
- unnecessary raw protocol-state material.

Raw projected values need not become public merely because the comparison result is protocol-relevant.

A digest, hash or commitment is not evidence of equivalence unless its canonicalization, authority and binding are defined by the published protocol.

Logs, diagnostics, database records, runtime reports, application responses and observability surfaces remain non-authoritative.

Any observable comparison result must:

- remain bounded to `U`;
- use neutral non-correlating representation;
- disclose no more than the published protocol requires;
- preserve action-bounded public identity.

Equivalent, material-conflict and indeterminate conditions must not become independently authoritative first-class protocol subjects.
